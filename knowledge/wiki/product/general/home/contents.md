# Home contents cover

Section 3 of the policy.

## What contents are

Any moveable household item belonging to the policyholder or a member of the
household, subject to an exclusion list
([source](../../../../raw/wordings/tiq-home-2025-03-15-v10.md#p2)).

The exclusions matter more than the definition, because they are where a
customer's assumption and the policy part company. They include property more
specifically insured under another policy; motor vehicles and accessories,
pedal cycles and watercraft; deeds, bonds, cheques, securities, stamps,
certificates, manuscripts, medals, contact lenses, hearing aids and livestock
unless specially mentioned; any part of the structure or ceilings, wallpaper,
external aerials and masts; and any property whose value is already included
in the sum insured for renovation, fixtures and fittings, or the building
([source](../../../../raw/wordings/tiq-home-2025-03-15-v10.md#p2)).

That last one is the overlap rule: an item counted under
[renovation](renovation.md) is not also contents.

## What is covered

Physical loss or damage to contents caused by any
[insured peril](../../../concept/insured-perils.md), for the policyholder and
the household, up to the sum insured stated in the schedule in aggregate for
any one policy year
([source](../../../../raw/wordings/tiq-home-2025-03-15-v10.md#p8)).

Valuables are defined separately and carry their own section
([source](../../../../raw/wordings/tiq-home-2025-03-15-v10.md#p4)).

## Why the spoken answer defers on specifics

The exclusion list is long and item-specific, and a caller asking about
contents usually has one object in mind. Reciting a general definition at
someone asking about their bicycle is how a bot gives a technically true
answer that misleads. The wording says pedal cycles are excluded; anything
less clear-cut goes to a person.
