# Renovation cover

Section 2 of the policy.

## What renovations are

Improvements and additions made within the home by the policyholder or by any
former owner, in the form of fixtures and fittings, which include flooring,
built-in wardrobes and kitchen cabinets, but which do not form any part of the
building
([source](../../../../raw/wordings/tiq-home-2025-03-15-v10.md#p4)).

The last clause is the one that decides arguments. Anything structural is
[building](building.md), not renovation, and the two have separate sums
insured.

## What is covered

Physical loss or damage to the renovation caused by any
[insured peril](../../../concept/insured-perils.md). Maximum liability in any
one policy year is the sum insured stated in the schedule, in aggregate
([source](../../../../raw/wordings/tiq-home-2025-03-15-v10.md#p8)).

Renovation using eco-friendly materials, given as VOC-free paint and
formaldehyde-free cabinets, is deemed covered by the same section
([source](../../../../raw/wordings/tiq-home-2025-03-15-v10.md#p8)).

## What the placeholder used to say

Before this wording was ingested, the bot said renovation cover applied to
"fixtures and fittings you have installed, such as flooring, built-in
cabinetry and wiring". Close, and wrong in two ways. Wiring is not in the
definition. Cover is not limited to work the current owner installed, since a
former owner's improvements count. The wording is now the source.
